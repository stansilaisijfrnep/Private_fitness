## Decision

**KEEP the calorie target at 3,300 kcal. No change.**

The weight is up 1.70 kg in seven days, which annualises to 2.1 % of bodyweight per week and is four
times the target rate. Taken alone that reads as "cut calories". It is the wrong reading, for three
reasons.

1. **He never ate the target.** Complete days averaged 2,892 kcal against a 3,300 target, and carbs
   averaged 344 g against 475 g. An estimated maintenance for 79 kg at 194 cm with five training
   sessions is roughly 3,000 to 3,300 kcal. He has been at or slightly below maintenance all week.
   A man cannot gain 1.7 kg of fat while eating at maintenance. Cutting calories now would cut a
   number he has not yet reached.
2. **The gain is water and glycogen.** He went from an intake he described as barely eating to
   300 to 440 g of carbohydrate a day. Every gram of stored glycogen binds roughly three grams of
   water. The single-day jump of +0.70 kg from 12 to 13 Sep followed the highest carb day of the
   block plus a salty McDonald's meal. That is not tissue.
3. **The trend is not yet measurable.** Two full weeks of daily readings are needed to compare
   rolling averages. This week has five readings and no previous week to compare against.

The decision is therefore not about the target but about hitting it. The real failure of week 1 was
the two days that collapsed: 9 Sep ended 1,000 kcal short after eating nothing between 13:15 and
21:45, and 11 Sep was never logged past breakfast.

**Reassess on 20 Sep with two clean weeks. If the 7-day average is still climbing faster than
0.5 % per week while he is actually eating 3,300, calories come down by 200.**

**The waist measurement will not happen.** Asked three times, he refused flatly on 13 Sep. That
decision is his and it is now recorded as permanent: never ask again. The consequence is not
neutral and he has been told it once. Without a waist measurement there is no signal separating
muscle from fat, so the calorie decision runs on rate of gain alone, and the threshold gets tighter
rather than looser: **if the 7-day average rises more than 0.5 % of bodyweight per week across two
clean weeks while he is actually eating 3,300, calories come down by 200.** A faster gain no longer
gets the benefit of the doubt, because nothing is left to justify it with.
