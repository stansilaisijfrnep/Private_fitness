## Asymmetry, reported 12 Sep
Asked whether the left knee collapsed inward or whether it was balance, he answered both: the knee wandered in and
out, and beyond the split squat he notices he lands slightly to the RIGHT off a box jump and stands slightly to the
RIGHT when he deadlifts. That is a systematic shift onto the stronger side, the classic pattern after a unilateral
knee operation: the body learned to offload the operated leg and kept the habit long after rehab ended.
No pain reported, so this is a training problem, not a medical one.

## What this session changed

**The left-right asymmetry finally showed up, and it is the headline.** The split squat with 8 kg
per hand: right side fine, left side gave way. He dropped to bodyweight himself. Asked to be
precise, he added that he lands slightly to the right off a box jump and stands slightly to the
right when he deadlifts. That is systematic offloading of the ACL-operated leg, invisible in every
bilateral lift he does. New standing rules: left leg leads every single-leg exercise, the right
matches its reps, load rises only when the left controls it, and both sides get reported every
session. Front-view filming and a left-right jump test added.

**Abs went from 0 to 3 hard sets in one session.** 13, 15, 15 hanging leg raises, all inside the
squat rest periods. The structural change worked exactly as intended: they were never skipped
because they were never last.

**The Copenhagen plank came out of the leg days.** His objection was correct: the leg press is heavy
and the adductors work in it. It sits on Upper and Pull now.

**Every load went up.** Squat 40 → 45 with depth held. Leg press 90 × 8 → 95 × 15. RDL 50 × 8 with a
drop to 40 → 50 × 12. New exercise, the hip thrust, straight to 80 kg.

**The hamstring work fell off the end again.** He mistook the leg extension for the leg curl and
finished without any seated leg curl. Third time the last exercise on a leg day has died, and this
time it cost the one muscle group that matters most for his knee. He then asked, unprompted, for
the next leg session to focus more on the posterior chain. Both point the same way: hamstring work
moves forward in the order.
